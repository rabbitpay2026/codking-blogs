      <div className="ck-summary"><div className="ck-summary-label">Summary</div><p>Releasit publishes two different COD apps on the Shopify App Store: a replacement order form with upsells, and a checkout extension for COD fees, advances and OTP. Only the second one overlaps with COD King, and comparing against the wrong one is how brands end up with the wrong tool. Plans and features below were read off the listings on 17 November 2026.</p></div>

      <div className="ck-body">
        <p className="ck-lede">We make COD King, so take the conclusions here with the scepticism you would apply to any vendor writing about a competitor. What we have tried to do instead is make the facts checkable: every claim about Releasit below comes from its own Shopify App Store listings, read on the day this was written, and where a listing does not mention something we say that rather than asserting an absence. The useful part of this comparison is not a verdict — it is the structural distinction that most comparison posts in this category get wrong.</p>

        <h2 id="two-apps">Releasit is two apps, and that is the whole comparison</h2>

        <p>Search for Releasit and you will find two separate listings from the same developer, doing substantially different jobs.</p>

        <p><strong>Releasit COD Form & Upsells</strong> describes itself as a one-click COD order form, offered as a popup or an embedded form, explicitly as an alternative to going through Shopify's checkout. Around that form it layers upsells, downsells, quantity offers, A/B testing and pixel tracking. On its listing, read on 17 November 2026, it shows 4.9 stars from 2,377 reviews, carries Shopify's Built for Shopify designation, lists a launch date of 31 August 2021, and supports twenty languages.</p>

        <p><strong>Releasit COD Fee</strong> is a different product. It is a checkout-side app whose listing describes adding fixed or percentage COD fees and discounts, collecting partial, split or deposit COD payments, requiring WhatsApp OTP verification, and showing or hiding COD by condition. Its listing shows 4.9 stars from 613 reviews, also carries Built for Shopify, lists a launch date of 9 July 2021, and supports nineteen languages.</p>

        <p>This matters because the two apps sit on opposite sides of the single most consequential decision in this category: whether your COD orders go through Shopify's own checkout or around it. COD King works inside Shopify's checkout. So, structurally, does Releasit COD Fee. Releasit COD Form is built for the other path. If you are weighing COD King against "Releasit" without knowing which listing you are looking at, you are not comparing two options — you are comparing two different architectures and a feature list.</p>

        <h2 id="shape">The shape decision comes before the feature list</h2>

        <p>A replacement order form gives you total control of the fields, the layout, the offer stack and the tracking. For a single-product funnel running cold paid traffic, that control is worth a great deal, and the upsell machinery attached to it is where the money is. The cost is that this path does not go through Shopify's checkout, which has consequences for Shop Pay, for native payment methods, for some apps that hook into checkout, and for how your analytics attribute the conversion.</p>

        <p>A checkout extension gives up that control entirely. You cannot restructure Shopify's checkout, you cannot put a four-step upsell ladder in it, and your form is Shopify's form. What you keep is everything else: Shop Pay, your existing gateways, your existing apps, and one checkout to reason about rather than two paths to maintain.</p>

        <p>We have written about this trade-off at length in <a href="/blog/native-checkout-vs-cod-order-form">native checkout versus a replacement COD form</a>, and the short version is that neither answer is correct in the abstract. It is decided by whether your revenue comes from an offer stack on cold traffic or from a catalogue sold to a returning base. Decide it first. It eliminates most of the market in one move, and it means you are then comparing apps that actually do the same job.</p>

        <h2 id="overlap">Where COD King and Releasit COD Fee actually overlap</h2>

        <p>Having narrowed it to the checkout-extension shape, the honest answer is that the overlap between COD King and Releasit COD Fee is substantial. Both listings describe COD fees, both describe collecting a partial payment up front, both describe conditional rules for when COD is offered, and both describe OTP verification over WhatsApp. If the feature names are all you look at, these two products look close to interchangeable.</p>

        <p>The differences are in where each one goes deeper, and they are worth setting out precisely.</p>

        <p><strong>What the Releasit COD Fee listing describes that is worth noting.</strong> Its conditional rules are stated in terms of country, product, collection, customer group and order value — a dimension set built for stores selling across several countries, where the catalogue and the market are the axes you want to slice on. Its partial-payment implementation is described in more operational detail than most: fixed or percentage advances, paid and due balances shown to the customer, and each payment tracked by date, method and amount. That payment-tracking detail is the kind of thing that only matters once you are reconciling advances against remittances at volume, and it is a real strength.</p>

        <p><strong>What COD King does that its listing does not cover.</strong> COD King's feature set, as described on our own site, extends past the checkout into the dispatch decision: a verification step that can confirm, hold or cancel a COD order before it ships; restriction of COD by high-risk location, customer and order; prepaid conversion offered as a discount rather than only as a fee on COD; prefilled addresses pulled from the phone number or past orders; branded WhatsApp and SMS for OTP and order updates; and abandoned-cart reminders over WhatsApp. The Releasit COD Fee listing does not mention a pre-dispatch hold, risk-based COD restriction, or prefilled addresses.</p>

        <p>One thing neither covers: <strong>pincode-level rules</strong>. Neither listing mentions pincodes or postal codes as a dimension for COD rules, and nor does our own site by that name. For an Indian brand this is the single most useful axis there is — your delivery performance varies enormously by pincode, far more than by order value — so if lane-level control is central to your plan, ask both vendors directly what granularity their location rules actually run at rather than inferring it from a feature list. We have written separately about <a href="/blog/cod-pincode-serviceability">how to build pincode logic</a> and what it is worth.</p>

        <p>Neither listing mentions NDR handling or courier integration, which is correct — that work sits in your shipping aggregator, not in a checkout app, and an app claiming to do it should be asked how.</p>

        <h2 id="pricing">Pricing: the order cap is the variable, not the headline price</h2>

        <p>Both Releasit apps are free to install, which as always tells you nothing. What actually determines your bill is the order cap on each tier, and the two apps count different things.</p>

        <p><strong>Releasit COD Fee</strong>, on its listing on 17 November 2026: a Forever Free tier covering up to 60 COD orders a month, including WhatsApp OTP, partial payments, COD fees and conditional COD rules; Premium at \$9.99 a month for up to 360 COD orders; and Enterprise at \$29.99 a month for unlimited COD orders, which adds technician setup and priority live chat. Annual billing is offered at a 25% discount on the Premium and Enterprise tiers.</p>

        <p><strong>Releasit COD Form & Upsells</strong> is priced on total orders rather than COD orders, and higher: Forever Free up to 60 orders a month, Premium at \$11.49 for up to 420, Enterprise at \$29.99 for up to 10,000, and Unlimited at \$69.99. The top tier is where the A/B testing and the AI product recommender sit.</p>

        <p>Two practical notes. First, all of those charges are billed in USD and, per the listings, recurring and usage-based charges are billed every 30 days — which for an Indian brand means an FX-exposed bill and a 30-day rather than calendar-month cycle. Second, the Free tier's 60-order ceiling is generous enough to run a genuine pilot on a small store and nowhere near enough for a brand at real volume, so price your comparison at your actual monthly COD order count, not at the tier you will start on.</p>

        <p>COD King is priced on usage rather than on monthly order tiers — our site states that you pay for what you use, with no setup fee, and the plan detail sits on our pricing page rather than in a tier table. That is a genuine point in Releasit's favour for anyone who wants a fixed, predictable monthly line item they can forecast: a capped plan at \$29.99 is easier to budget than a usage-based bill, and if budget predictability matters more to you than the extra capability, that is a legitimate reason to choose it.</p>

        <h2 id="messaging">The messaging bill nobody puts in the comparison</h2>

        <p>Whichever app you pick, the plan price is frequently not the largest number. OTP and confirmation messages cost money per message, and at volume that line will exceed your app subscription.</p>

        <p>The Releasit COD Form listing is explicit that SMS messages are optional and charged separately. That is the normal arrangement across this category, and the question to resolve before you commit is which model each app uses: messaging included in the plan at a bundled or per-message rate, or bring-your-own provider where you connect an account and are billed by the provider directly. Bring-your-own is cheaper at volume and more work to set up; included messaging is simpler and usually dearer per message.</p>

        <p>Work out your cost per verified order at your actual volume under each model before you compare plan prices. A brand verifying 4,000 COD orders a month will find that the difference between the two messaging models dwarfs the difference between a \$9.99 and a \$29.99 subscription. Our breakdown of <a href="/blog/whatsapp-vs-sms-vs-ivr-cod-verification">what WhatsApp, SMS and IVR each cost and require</a> has the mechanics, including the DLT registration that Indian SMS and voice both need.</p>

        <h2 id="decide">How to decide between them in practice</h2>

        <p>Four questions settle it faster than a feature matrix.</p>

        <p><strong>Does your revenue come from an offer stack or a catalogue?</strong> Offer stack on cold traffic points to the replacement-form shape, and Releasit COD Form is the strongest app of that shape by review volume. Catalogue sold to a returning base points to a checkout extension, and the form apps are then the wrong group entirely.</p>

        <p><strong>Is your problem priced cash, or bad orders?</strong> If it is purely that COD is too cheap for the customer and too expensive for you, the problem is solved by fees and advances, and both apps do that. If it is that a meaningful share of your COD orders should never be dispatched at all, you need a verification step that can hold an order before it ships, and that is a narrower set of tools.</p>

        <p><strong>How many countries do you sell into?</strong> One market, India, with lane-level variance as your main cost driver: a single-market app will go deeper where it matters. Several COD markets from one backend: Releasit's rule dimensions and nineteen-to-twenty-language coverage are built for exactly that, and are a better fit than we are.</p>

        <p><strong>Do you need the bill to be predictable?</strong> If a fixed monthly cap is a requirement rather than a preference, that favours a tiered plan.</p>

        <h2 id="releasit-better">Where Releasit is the better choice</h2>

        <p>Stated plainly, because a comparison in which the author wins on every axis is not worth reading.</p>

        <p>If you want a custom one-page COD form instead of Shopify's checkout, with upsells, downsells and quantity offers around it, Releasit COD Form is built for that and we are not — we work inside Shopify's checkout by design. If you sell into several COD markets and need rules by country and collection plus deep multi-language coverage, Releasit fits that better than a single-market app. If you want a predictable capped monthly subscription, the tiered plans are easier to budget than usage-based pricing. And if your requirement is detailed tracking of split COD payments against balances, the COD Fee listing describes that specifically.</p>

        <p>Both Releasit apps also carry Shopify's Built for Shopify designation and a long review history going back to 2021, which is a legitimate signal about build quality and longevity that any newer app has to earn rather than claim.</p>

        <h2 id="not-us">When COD King is the wrong choice</h2>

        <p>If you need a replacement checkout form, we do not do that. If IVR voice verification is central to your plan, we do not offer it, and our own site names OTP, WhatsApp and SMS only. If your main market is not India, our depth is in the wrong place. If pincode-level lane control is the thing you are buying for, ask us what granularity we actually run at before you install, and hold us to the answer. And if you are doing a handful of COD orders a day, you probably do not need any app in this category yet — manual confirmation over WhatsApp costs nothing and works fine until volume makes it impractical.</p>

        <h2 id="test">Settle it with two weeks of your own data</h2>

        <p>Both apps have a free tier that covers 60 orders a month, which is enough to run a real test on a small store and enough to validate the setup on a large one. Install the two that survived the questions above, point a defined slice of traffic at each, and measure four things against your own baseline: OTP response rate, the share of orders confirmed before dispatch, RTO rate on dispatched orders, and prepaid share. Two weeks of your own numbers will settle the question in a way that no comparison article — including this one — can, because it is the only method that accounts for your catalogue, your price points and your customers.</p>

        <p>One caution on the test: do not change your COD rules and your app in the same fortnight. If you tighten your order-value floor at the same time as you install a verification tool, you will not be able to attribute the result to either.</p>

        <h2 id="short">The short version</h2>

        <p>Releasit publishes two COD apps that do different jobs. The form app replaces Shopify's checkout and is built around upsells; the fee app works at checkout and overlaps genuinely with what we do. Decide the checkout-shape question first, because it eliminates one of them outright. If you land on the checkout-extension side, the two products are close on fees, advances, conditional rules and WhatsApp OTP; Releasit goes deeper on multi-country rule dimensions, split-payment tracking and predictable tiered pricing, while our depth is in the pre-dispatch decision — holding or cancelling risky COD orders, restricting COD by risk, prefilled addresses and prepaid conversion. Price both at your real monthly COD volume, include the messaging bill, and then test for a fortnight.</p>

        <p>If you are an Indian D2C brand on Shopify's native checkout whose problem is some mix of fake orders, RTO and a payment mix tilted too far toward cash, that is the case COD King is built for — you can see the feature detail and pricing at <a href="https://codking.tech" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">codking.tech</a>. If your problem is an upsell funnel on a custom form, go and read Releasit's listing instead; it is the better tool for that job.</p>
