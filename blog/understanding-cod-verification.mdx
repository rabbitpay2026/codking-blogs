A merchant may begin packing and shipping before receiving payment, so confirming that the customer genuinely intends to receive the order can be valuable.</p><h2>What COD verification means</h2><p>COD verification is a confirmation step between order placement and fulfillment. Depending on the store workflow, confirmation may happen through an OTP, a customer response, or another explicit action. The goal is not to create unnecessary friction; it is to distinguish stronger purchase intent from orders that may need attention.</p><h2>Where verification fits in the order flow</h2><p>A useful verification flow happens early enough to influence fulfillment decisions. The store receives the COD order, requests confirmation, records the outcome, and then applies its normal fulfillment rules.</p><h2>Designing a customer-friendly verification experience</h2><p>Keep instructions short, make the requested action obvious, and explain why confirmation is needed. Mobile usability matters because many shoppers complete the entire purchase journey on a phone.</p><h2>Key takeaways</h2><ul><li>COD verification helps establish customer intent before fulfillment.</li><li>The confirmation step should be simple, timely, and mobile-friendly.</li><li>Verification should support operational decisions rather than add friction.</li></ul><h2>Conclusion</h2><p>COD verification works best as one part of a broader order-quality process. Clear confirmation and consistent customer communication can help teams make better fulfillment decisions.</p></section></div>
